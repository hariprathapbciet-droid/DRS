Help & Support

For account, image upload or screening-related technical questions.

What is Retinopathy?

Retinopathy refers to damage or changes affecting the retina, which is the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye. The retina receives light and sends visual information to the brain.

Retinal changes can develop gradually. Some people may not notice symptoms during the early stages. Regular eye examinations can help identify retinal changes at an early stage.

What is Diabetic Retinopathy?

Diabetic retinopathy is an eye condition associated with diabetes. Over time, high blood sugar levels can damage the small blood vessels that supply the retina.

How does it affect the eye?

Damage to the retinal blood vessels can cause changes in the retina. If the condition progresses, these changes can affect vision. Early diabetic retinopathy may not cause noticeable symptoms, making regular eye screening important for people with diabetes.

Possible Warning Signs

  • Blurred or changing vision
  • Difficulty seeing clearly
  • Changes in vision that come and go
  • Difficulty seeing at night

These symptoms can have different causes. Anyone experiencing changes in vision should consult a qualified eye-care professional.

What is Retinopathy Screening?

Retinopathy screening is an examination used to look for changes in the retina. A retinal or fundus image can provide information about the appearance of the back of the eye.

Why is screening important?

Some retinal changes may develop before noticeable vision problems occur. Screening can help identify people who may need further examination by an eye-care professional.

How does DRS work?

  • Capture or upload a suitable retinal image.
  • The system processes the uploaded image.
  • The screening system analyzes the image for patterns associated with retinal changes.
  • A screening result is displayed.
  • If an abnormality is suspected, professional eye evaluation is recommended.

Taking Care of Your Eye Health

Taking care of your overall health can help reduce the risk of diabetes-related eye complications. People with diabetes should follow the advice of their healthcare team and attend recommended eye examinations.

Important Eye-Care Tips

  • Attend recommended eye examinations.
  • Follow your healthcare professional's advice for diabetes management.
  • Report new or sudden changes in vision to an eye-care professional.
  • Keep your regular health check-ups.
  • Do not rely only on an online screening result for diagnosis.

Important Information

DRS is designed as a screening and awareness platform. A screening result is not a medical diagnosis. If you have concerns about your vision or eye health, consult a qualified eye-care professional.
